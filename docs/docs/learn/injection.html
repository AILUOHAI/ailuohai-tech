Injection layouts

Layouts differ in where fuel is delivered, at what pressure and when in the cycle.

Injector in the intake port

Pressure: typically 3–6 bar.

Fuel is delivered into the intake port ahead of the valve, usually before the intake stroke. The mixture has time to prepare, but part of the fuel forms a film on the port walls.

  • Pros: simple fuel system, good homogenization, fuel washes the valves.
  • Cons: transport delay from the wall film, less charge cooling.

Injector in the cylinder

Pressure: typically 50–350 bar.

Fuel is delivered straight into the combustion chamber. Pressure comes from a cam-driven high-pressure pump; the injector is opened by a driver with a high-voltage current peak and a hold phase.

  • Pros: precise fuel per cycle, multiple injections, charge cooling and a larger knock margin.
  • Cons: complex driver and pump, carbon build-up on intake valves, limited mixing time at high rpm.

Two injectors per cylinder

Pressure: each injector has its own.

For each cylinder the ECU splits the fuel per cycle between the GDI and PFI injectors depending on the operating point.

  • Pros: extra flow capacity, clean valves, flexibility across operating points.
  • Cons: two fuel systems and an additional split calibration.
