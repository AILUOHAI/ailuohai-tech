Air and fuel In progress

Cylinder filling

Air mass is calculated from intake pressure with a linear model: slope and offset depend on rpm and on intake and exhaust cam positions, with a temperature correction. Each bank is calculated separately because each has its own cam phases. The model is easy to invert: from the required air mass the unit gets the required intake pressure and, through the throttle flow model, the throttle angle.

Fuel

Fuel mass per cylinder = air mass / (stoichiometric ratio × target lambda). Target lambda comes from the base map, start and warm-up corrections, component protection enrichment and the wideband correction.

Hybrid injection

For each cylinder the calculated fuel is split between the direct injector and the port injector. Direct injection can use 1–3 pulses per cycle with a high-voltage current profile from the calibration; start of injection depends on rpm and load. Injector open time is calculated from the injector model for the actual pressure difference.

Rail pressure

Two high-pressure pumps, normally one per bank. Target pressure is a 32×32 map of rpm and load; control combines feed-forward from fuel demand with a rail model and integral action near the target.

Valve timing

Targets for four cams are maps of rpm and load. Control is allowed only with enough oil pressure; on low oil pressure the cams return to the base position. Valve circuit open and short faults, failure to reach target and a shifted rest position are diagnosed.

From torque to throttle

Torque becomes filling, then intake pressure, flow through the throttle and its angle: through physics rather than a lever-to-percent table.

Mixture and wideband correction

Target lambda comes from a map with warm-up and exhaust protection corrections. Three independent wideband modes run: PI, fast cell correction and long-term adaptation.

Fuel and pulse

Fuel per cycle is split between PFI and GDI (up to three pulses). Each part's time comes from the injector model at the actual pressure difference.
