Torque-based control

How the lever becomes air and spark

Torque-based control In progress

The unit does not convert the throttle lever directly into throttle opening. The lever is a torque request. The model decides how to deliver this torque — with air and spark timing — and all limits act on torque. This is the same principle as in modern OEM engine management, adapted to an airboat: engine, gearbox and a heavy propeller.

Model levels

  1. Position and time — crankshaft and four camshafts give the engine angle and cam phases.
  2. Sensors — every value has a validity flag; a failed sensor is replaced by a substitute or model value.
  3. Engine model — cylinder filling, residual gas, spark and mixture efficiency, losses, exhaust temperature.
  4. Torque structure — request, coordination, limits, split into air and spark.
  5. Actuator targets — throttle, cam phases, fuel mass, injection timing and split, rail pressure, spark.
  6. Outputs — angle-based scheduling on hardware timers drives injectors and coils.

At idle the air path keeps a small torque reserve above the target, and spark timing uses it. This lets the idle react instantly, without waiting for the intake manifold to fill. The actual torque is calculated forward the same way and used for supervision and logs.

Torque structure

The lever and idle controller request torque, limits take the minimum, and two paths deliver it: air slowly and spark quickly. That is why limits are smooth, with no cuts.

Torque reserve

More air is held than needed and late spark absorbs the excess. When torque is needed at once, spark returns to optimum within one cycle, without waiting for the manifold to fill.
